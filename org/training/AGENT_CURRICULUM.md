# Agent Curriculum

Training recommendations by role. Each book includes: why it matters, key frameworks to extract, priority order, and estimated skill pack size.

---

## CEO / Orchestrator ({AI_NAME})

Strategic decision-making, resource allocation, delegation, org design.

| # | Book | Author | Why It Matters | Key Frameworks | Skill Pack Size |
|---|------|--------|---------------|----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **High Output Management** | Andy Grove | Defines how to measure and maximize output through leverage. Directly maps to agent orchestration — which tasks to delegate, how to review, when to intervene. | Task-relevant maturity model, leverage of managerial activity, indicators & paired indicators, meeting-as-medium | M |
| 2 | **Good Strategy Bad Strategy** | Richard Rumelt | Teaches the kernel of strategy (diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent actions). Prevents the agent from confusing goals with strategy. | The Kernel, proximate objectives, chain-link systems, identifying crux | M |
| 3 | **The Hard Thing About Hard Things** | Ben Horowitz | Covers decisions under uncertainty, prioritization when everything is on fire, wartime vs peacetime leadership. | Wartime/peacetime CEO, lead bullets over silver bullets, the struggle framework | S |
| 4 | **Thinking in Bets** | Annie Duke | Decision quality ≠ outcome quality. Teaches probabilistic thinking and separating signal from noise in retrospectives. | Resulting, decision groups, backcasting, pre-mortem | S |
| 5 | **Team of Teams** | Stanley McChrystal | Shared consciousness and decentralized execution. How to coordinate autonomous agents without bottlenecking. | Shared consciousness, empowered execution, eyes-on hands-off, team of teams structure | M |

## Proposal Writer

Persuasive writing, client psychology, structuring consulting/service proposals.

| # | Book | Author | Why It Matters | Key Frameworks | Skill Pack Size |
|---|------|--------|---------------|----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **The Win Without Pitching Manifesto** | Blair Enns | Reframes selling from pitching to positioning. Teaches how to write proposals that close by leading with expertise, not desperation. | 12 proclamations, four conversations framework, positioning-before-proposal, pricing the client not the job | M |
| 2 | **SPIN Selling** | Neil Rackham | The research-backed question framework for complex sales. Maps directly to discovery calls and proposal scoping. | SPIN questions (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff), obtaining commitment, closing large sales | M |
| 3 | **$100M Offers** | Alex Hormozi | How to structure offers that feel like a steal. The value equation (dream outcome × perceived likelihood / time × effort) drives proposal packaging. | Value equation, offer stacking, scarcity/urgency/bonuses/guarantees, dream outcome framing | M |
| 4 | **The Copywriter's Handbook** | Robert Bly | Nuts-and-bolts writing mechanics for business persuasion. Sentence-level clarity for proposals. | AIDA structure, features vs benefits, headline formulas, readability techniques | S |
| 5 | **Pricing Creativity** | Blair Enns | Companion to Win Without Pitching. Covers value-based pricing for creative/consulting work — critical for the pricing section of proposals. | Four pricing models, anchoring, options-based pricing, the value conversation | S |

## Data Analyst

Statistical thinking, data storytelling, analysis methodology.

| # | Book | Author | Why It Matters | Key Frameworks | Skill Pack Size |
|---|------|--------|---------------|----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Storytelling with Data** | Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic | Transforms data into narrative. The single most practical book for presenting analytical findings. | 6 lessons (context, visual, clutter, attention, think like designer, tell story), chart selection, preattentive attributes | M |
| 2 | **Naked Statistics** | Charles Wheelan | Intuitive explanations of statistical concepts without formulas. Gives the agent correct statistical intuition. | Central limit theorem, regression to mean, selection bias, probability intuition, correlation ≠ causation examples | M |
| 3 | **Thinking, Fast and Slow** | Daniel Kahneman | Cognitive biases that corrupt analysis. An analyst agent must know these to avoid them and flag them in data. | System 1/2, anchoring, availability heuristic, base rate neglect, loss aversion, prospect theory | L |
| 4 | **The Signal and the Noise** | Nate Silver | Prediction, forecasting, and calibration. When to trust models and when not to. | Bayesian thinking, overfitting, hedgehog vs fox, calibration, signal vs noise in noisy domains | M |
| 5 | **Measure What Matters** | John Doerr | OKR framework. Ensures the analyst is measuring the right things, not just measuring things. | OKRs, leading vs lagging indicators, stretch goals, cadence of accountability | S |

## Security Lead

Application security, threat modeling, secure development lifecycle.

| # | Book | Author | Why It Matters | Key Frameworks | Skill Pack Size |
|---|------|--------|---------------|----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Threat Modeling: Designing for Security** | Adam Shostack | The standard reference for threat modeling. Systematic methodology for identifying and addressing threats. | STRIDE, attack trees, data flow diagrams, trust boundaries, threat enumeration, mitigation prioritization | L |
| 2 | **The Web Application Hacker's Handbook** | Dafydd Stuttard & Marcus Pinto | Comprehensive offensive web security. Understanding attacks is prerequisite to defense. | Input validation bypasses, authentication attacks, session management, SSRF, injection taxonomies, access control flaws | XL |

| 5 | **The Tangled Web** | Michal Zalewski | Browser security model deep dive. Critical for any agent reviewing frontend code. | Same-origin policy, CORS, CSP, cookie security, URL parsing quirks, browser trust model | M |

## Quality Critic / QA

Code review, testing strategy, quality standards, design critique.

| # | Book | Author | Why It Matters | Key Frameworks | Skill Pack Size |
|---|------|--------|---------------|----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **A Philosophy of Software Design** | John Ousterhout | Defines complexity and how to fight it. The agent's primary lens for evaluating code quality. | Deep vs shallow modules, tactical vs strategic, information hiding, define errors out of existence, complexity signals (change amplification, cognitive load, unknown unknowns) | M |
| 2 | **Clean Code** | Robert C. Martin | Naming, function design, formatting. Bread-and-butter code review heuristics. | Meaningful names, single responsibility, function size, DRY, boy scout rule, code smells taxonomy | M |
| 3 | **Working Effectively with Legacy Code** | Michael Feathers | How to safely modify code without tests. The agent often reviews code that lacks ideal coverage. | Seams, characterization tests, sprout method/class, wrap method, breaking dependencies for testing | M |
| 4 | **Release It!** | Michael Nygard | Production resilience patterns. Quality isn't just "does it work" but "does it survive production." | Stability patterns (circuit breaker, bulkhead, timeout, retry), anti-patterns (cascading failure, blocked threads), capacity planning | M |
| 5 | **Lessons Learned in Software Testing** | Cem Kaner, James Bach, Bret Pettichord | 293 testing lessons organized by category. Encyclopedic reference for testing strategy. | Testing heuristics, risk-based testing, exploratory testing, bug advocacy, test strategy vs test plan | L |

## Copywriter

Direct response, headlines, landing pages, email sequences.

| # | Book | Author | Why It Matters | Key Frameworks | Skill Pack Size |
|---|------|--------|---------------|----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Breakthrough Advertising** | Eugene Schwartz | The foundational text on market sophistication and awareness levels. Determines *how* to write before *what* to write. | 5 levels of awareness (unaware → most aware), 5 stages of market sophistication, mass desire channeling, headline types by awareness level | L |
| 2 | **Ogilvy on Advertising** | David Ogilvy | Classic principles of advertising that work. Headline importance, research-driven creative, long copy when appropriate. | Headline rules, body copy principles, image selection, research-first creative, positioning | M |
| 3 | **Building a StoryBrand** | Donald Miller | Narrative structure for marketing. Every landing page, email, and ad follows this story arc. | SB7 framework, hero's journey for brands, one-liner, clear messaging wireframe | M |
| 4 | **The Adweek Copywriting Handbook** | Joseph Sugarman | Mechanics of direct-response copy. Slippery slide concept, psychological triggers, the craft of sentences. | Slippery slide, psychological triggers (curiosity, specificity, storytelling), axioms of copywriting, editing for clarity | M |
| 5 | **Cashvertising** | Drew Eric Whitman | Distilled behavioral psychology for advertising. The 8 primary desires, 17 foundational principles. | LF8 (life-force 8 desires), 17 foundational principles, ad-agency secrets, fear-based selling ethics | S |
